# The Ferryman Game Design and Prototype Workbook

Editable examples and templates for the whole game

Version 0.2 | 17 September 2026 | Team F2P

The next task is to test a complete crossing, then a short run, and use what happens to decide the larger game. This workbook covers the concept, destinations, rules, content, presentation, digital systems, and playtesting. Completed examples provide something concrete to try. Blank templates let the team replace those examples and extend the design.

**Decision authority:** The team owns the final experience, destination rules, morality, progression, ending, platform, engine, art direction, and production scope. Using an example in a test does not approve it for the final game.

## Quick summary

**The game:** Play Charon. Choose who fits in a four-seat boat and where to take them. Paying souls support the service. Souls also leave memories that affect later crossings. Those left waiting can become wraiths. The destination system is a new idea to test alongside the report's core loop.

**The central choice:** Compare money, relationships, future memories, destination, and the cost of refusal. The design aims to make these considerations compete. Playtesting must establish whether they do.

**A crossing:** Refill shore, recall memories, choose route and passengers, resolve pressure, collect rewards, pay any quota, advance refusal, and clean up.

**Playable example:** Six crossings. Start with two obols and three Standing. Refill to five souls. Carry at most four seats. Play at most one residue for one Standing. Pay three obols after crossings three and six. Three refusals create a wraith. Failure occurs if pressure exceeds Standing or a quota cannot be paid.

**Three trial routes:** Elysium costs one obol and has zero base pressure. Asphodel is free and has one pressure. Tartarus is free and has two pressure, with one service obol on a nonempty arrival. These settings test a tradeoff; they do not define a soul's moral worth.

**Greek myth directions:** Explore divine judgments, guides, gates, oaths, memory, and seasonal passage. Source notes and distinct gameplay adaptations appear in section 20.

**Team choices still open:** Charon's authority, soul wishes, final route rules, the meaning of Standing, deck management, failure and endings, progression, platform, engine, and presentation.

**Next checkpoint:** Prepare the coming week's paper-prototype showcase using section 17A. Confirm the date and demo length, prepare the kit, rehearse, and complete checkpoint C01 before presenting.

**Start here:** Make the twelve soul slips and residue cards in sections 9 and 10. Follow sections 6 to 8. Record one run using section 18. Treat every added rule as provisional until the team reviews the evidence.

## 5 Explore the underworld destinations

The destination idea can change the core question from only who boards to who travels together and where they go. The location should affect a choice the player can understand before committing. These identities and mechanics are fictional design proposals.

### Three viable routing models

| Model | What the player chooses | What it adds | Cost to the design |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One destination per crossing | A route and one boat composition | Competing passenger needs within the current loop | Risk of repetitive grouping by destination |
| Multiple stops | An itinerary using a shared travel budget | Route order, disembarking, and mixed passengers | More time, states, and exceptions; capacity may matter less |
| Conditional passage | Whether to use a paid or temporary opening | Anticipation and memorable opportunities | Requires clear signals; inaccessible routes can frustrate |

**TRIAL choice:** Start with one destination per crossing. Test a timed opening later while keeping the same boarding rules. This makes the effect of each addition easier to observe.

### Destination concepts to develop

**Elysium, preservation.** A quiet destination where a cherished memory remains vivid. One soul seeks peace; another fears being held inside an idealized past. Possible later mechanic: preserve a memory or repeat its effect. Possible access: a paid passage permit, a patron's invitation, or a brief opening. The team decides who may enter and whether anyone can refuse.

**Asphodel, release and belonging.** A destination where individual distinctions soften. Anonymity can be relief for one passenger and loss for another. Possible later mechanic: share or redistribute an effect among souls. Possible access: dependable public passage. The team decides whether this is the regular route and what arrival means for identity.

**Tartarus, reckoning.** A destination for confronting unfinished obligations. A witness, companion, or accountable figure may have a reason to travel there. Possible later mechanic: accept a known obligation now and resolve it later. Possible access: a commission, summons, escort contract, or temporary opening. The team decides whether Charon is enforcing, witnessing, or helping someone seek redress.

These concepts do not assign souls a numerical moral worth. If the team wants judgment to be part of play, specify who judges, what the player knows, and how disagreement becomes an action.

## 7 Game Master procedure

Use this sequence without adding discretionary rulings. If a rule is missing, write the question down and agree a temporary ruling visibly before continuing.

1. **Shore.** Keep waiting souls and their refusal counters. Refill available spaces to five using the next supply cards. New souls start at zero refusals. Wraiths are separate and never occupy a shore slot. When supply is exhausted, the shore may contain fewer than five souls.
2. **Recall.** Draw up to two residue cards from the front of the queue. If it empties, move discard cards into the queue in oldest-discarded-first order. Do not shuffle. If fewer than two cards exist, draw only those available.
3. **Load.** Choose the route, passengers, and optional residue. Confirm all targets and validate capacity and payment. Pay the route toll from current obols and the residue cost from current Standing. Future fares cannot cover these costs.
4. **Cross.** Read aboard effects from the locked boat. Each applies once. Pressure equals route pressure plus existing wraiths plus conflict modifiers, minus protection. Clamp pressure to zero. If Standing is at least pressure, subtract it and continue. Otherwise end before arrival with no delivery rewards.
5. **Arrive.** Collect printed fares and Standing yields from delivered souls. Collect any route service payment. Select solo or joint residue variants from the boat's composition. Put each new residue card at the bottom of the draw queue in ascending soul ID order. It cannot be played this crossing. Record every soul's destination in the delivery history.
6. **Quota.** On crossings three and six, pay three obols. If the bank contains fewer than three, record quota failure. Arrival income is available for this payment.
7. **Decay.** Every soul left on the shore receives one normal refusal unless protected from normal decay. Apply any extra separation refusal. At three or more refusals, flip the soul into the wraith area. New wraiths first add pressure next crossing.
8. **Cleanup.** Put the played residue in discard first, then unplayed hand cards in left-to-right order. Clear the boat, retaining its delivered souls in the history area. Advance the crossing counter. End after crossing six if no earlier failure occurred.

### Timing rules that settle common disputes

- The opening deck is empty. The first crossing therefore has no residue play.
- A soul arriving during Shore can receive its first refusal during that crossing's Decay.
- A linked pair yields its joint residue only when both arrive on the same crossing.
- Extra separation decay occurs only when both linked souls were on the shore at departure and exactly one boards. Add one extra refusal to the waiting partner, once.
- A partner still in supply, already delivered, or already a wraith cannot trigger separation decay. Later delivery never upgrades an old residue card.
- Protection prevents pressure; it never restores Standing. Resources cannot become negative.
- Wraiths persist, cannot board, and yield no fares or residue in this trial.
- Empty crossings still incur route costs, pressure, quotas, and decay. They receive no Tartarus service payment.
- There are no purchases, discards by choice, culling, route changes, or reactive plays outside Load.

## 8 Worked crossing and boundary examples

### A complete first crossing

**TRIAL setup:** The shore contains S01 Mother, S02 Child, S03 Merchant, S04 Red Soldier, and S05 Poet. The bank has two obols and three Standing. No residue or wraiths exist.

**Choice:** Take Mother, Child, and Poet to Asphodel. Their seat costs are 2 + 1 + 1 = 4. Pay no route toll and play no residue.

**Cross:** Asphodel contributes one pressure. Poet grants one protection because two other souls are aboard. Pressure is max(0, 1 - 1) = 0. Standing stays at three.

**Arrive:** All three souls have zero fare. Each yields one Standing, so Standing becomes six. Mother and Child each produce R05 Joined Memory; Poet produces R03 Recollection. Add these in S01, S02, S05 order. The obol bank stays at two.

**Decay and cleanup:** Merchant and Red Soldier each gain one refusal. No quota is due. At the next Shore, add S06, S07, and S08 to return the shore to five. The next hand contains the two R05 cards. This choice preserved a family and built Standing, but earned no money toward the first quota.

### Four edge examples

**Separation:** Mother waits at one refusal while Child boards. With both available at departure, Mother gains one normal and one extra refusal, reaching three. She becomes a wraith after arrival and contributes pressure starting next crossing.

**Insufficient toll:** Elysium costs one banked obol. With zero obols at departure, it is unavailable even if every selected passenger carries a fare. Choose an affordable route before locking the boat.

**Standing timing:** With two Standing, playing one residue leaves one before pressure. A final pressure of one is survivable and leaves zero. A final pressure of two ends the run before fare and Standing rewards can arrive.

**No supply:** If all twelve souls have left the supply, refill adds nothing. Available souls can still board. An empty shore does not cancel the remaining crossing, its quota, or existing wraith pressure.

## 10 Residue and deck mechanics

The report's intent is a deck that records the carried dead rather than a conventional power ladder. [R, p. 1] The trial uses a queue to make each test reproducible. A random deck, a visible tableau, and active memories with an archive remain team alternatives.

### Complete residue examples

**TRIAL common rule:** Pay one Standing and commit at most one card during Load. Name any target before confirmation. Resolve its effect during Cross or Decay as stated. The card goes to discard during Cleanup, whether its conditional effect succeeds or not.

| ID and name | Exact effect | Source in trial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R01 Steadiness | Grant 2 protection during Cross | S09 and S10 |
| R02 Vigil | Grant 3 protection if at least one passenger has printed seat cost 2; otherwise grant 0 | S03 and S08 |
| R03 Recollection | Name one soul staying ashore. Prevent its one normal refusal this crossing. Extra separation refusal still applies | S05 and S07 |

R05 intentionally rewards carrying a pair together. R06 is a diminished variant. This is an explicit trial interpretation of the report's linked-residue idea, not evidence that any resulting deck is balanced.

### Card movement and targeting

Draw queue -> hand -> committed card -> discard -> draw queue. Newly delivered residue enters the bottom of the draw queue. Source-soul history remains in a separate ledger even if a future rule removes its active card.

For R03 or R05, select a legal waiting soul if one exists. If the shore will be empty, the decay-prevention part has no effect; the card remains playable. A card never shields an already active wraith. Prevention lasts only for the current crossing. Multiple protection sources add together before pressure is clamped.

## 12 System specification and economy

### M01 Destination wishes

A token created on crossing six appears as an unresolved obligation in the final history; it does not extend the trial.

**TEAM DECISION:** Should destination transform every memory, a few special souls, or only narrative text? The trial's narrow mapping limits the content required to answer that question.

### M04 Appeasing a wraith

**Purpose:** Test a recovery action. After Recall and before Load, once per crossing, pay two Standing to move one existing wraith to a Released area. Remove its pressure contribution immediately. This uses no boat seat and produces no fare, Standing reward, or residue.

The payment must be affordable before any arrival. Newly transformed wraiths can be targeted only on a later crossing. If no active wraith exists, the action is unavailable. Record the original soul and release crossing; do not erase its waiting history.

**Example:** With three Standing and two wraiths, appease one. Standing becomes one and one wraith remains. A subsequent free Asphodel crossing has two pressure before protection, so recovery does not automatically make the voyage safe.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is release a ritual, restitution, memory, or delivery? Should the cost be a resource, a seat, or a specific relationship? Test whether appeasement is a meaningful choice or routine maintenance.

### M05 Archiving or forgetting

**Purpose:** Test deck clutter and the meaning of letting go. After a successfully paid Quota step, or the skipped Quota step on other crossings, once per run, choose one residue instance from the draw queue or discard. Put it in an Archive and gain two Standing. A hand or committed card is not eligible. Preserve the remaining queue order and the delivered soul's ledger entry.

**Reversible trial:** On a later crossing, after Recall and before Load, the player may pay two Standing once to return that instance to the bottom of the queue. It cannot be drawn or played immediately. Retrieval gives no reward and does not restore the once-per-run archive action.

**Alternative permanent trial:** Omit retrieval entirely. Do not switch variants during a run. No eligible card means the action waits unused. Since archiving occurs after arrival and quota, it cannot rescue a failed crossing or an unpaid quota.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is the card merely inactive, or is a memory truly lost? Keep the historical name visible under either interpretation unless the team deliberately chooses otherwise.

### M06 Quota debt and escalation

**Debt trial:** At the crossing-three quota only, if the bank is below three, pay all available obols and record the missing amount plus one as debt. The run continues. That full debt is due after crossing-four arrival, before Decay. If it cannot be paid, end the run. Crossing six still requires three obols in full; no extension beyond the trial is allowed.

Example: Having one obol at crossing three pays one and creates three debt: two missing plus one surcharge. Arrival income on crossing four can repay it. Debt never removes Standing and cannot be repaid with residue.

**Alternative escalation trial:** Keep immediate quota failure, but change the two announced quotas to three and four. Do not use debt in the same first test. This isolates the effect of the rising requirement.

**TEAM DECISION:** Should Hades create a survival deadline, an obligation that can be negotiated, or an endless duty? Choose the consequence of missing a quota before adding a long campaign.

### M07 Boat development and supplies

**Capacity trial:** At the start of crossing four, before refill, offer one permanent extra seat for three banked obols. Buying it makes capacity five for crossings four to six. Declining removes the offer for this run. Capacity never changes during a locked crossing. Keep printed two-seat conditions unchanged.

**Alternative supplies trial:** Keep capacity four. During Load, permit one purchase of a one-crossing ward for one obol. It grants one protection, adds to residue and aboard protection, and expires after Cross. It is paid at confirmation, cannot be stored, and has no refund on failure.

These are separate tests because both spend money on crossing safety. Neither is active in the basic rules. **TEAM DECISION:** Does progression expand power, add lateral options, or reveal more situations? The report defers changing capacity; adding upgrades is a deliberate scope change. [R, pp. 2 to 3]

### M08 River uncertainty

**Purpose:** Test hidden resolution only after visible events are understandable. Before the run, show the four possible event rules, shuffle those cards once, and conceal their order. Draw one after Load is locked on each of crossings one to four. Do not recycle the event pile. Crossings five and six have no event.

Use E01, E02 and E04 from section 11. Replace E03 with **Late Invitation:** on a successful Elysium arrival, refund the one ordinary route-toll obol paid that crossing; otherwise no effect. No other payment is refunded. This replaces a pre-departure discount that could not sensibly operate after commitment.

Event pressure is applied before protection. There is no reactive residue play, passenger swap, or rerouting. Show a pressure range before confirmation, not a guaranteed total. Keep soul rewards visible.

**TEAM DECISION:** Hidden draw, current-event preview, or future-event preview? Record whether surprise creates anticipation, confusion, or choices with no plausible counterplay. Do not combine hidden events with hidden soul rewards in the first test.

### M09 Uncertain soul memories

**Purpose:** Test uncertainty with a disclosed possibility range. Keep every statistic except S03's residue visible. At setup, randomly assign S03 either R02 or R03, with one of each equally available before the assignment. Conceal the result; print both possibilities on the soul card. Reveal and acquire the assigned card only on successful arrival.

S03's seats, fare, Standing, and eligibility never change. If it becomes a wraith or the crossing fails, it grants no residue and need not reveal. Keep river events off for this comparison.

**TEAM DECISION:** What signal allows inference about hidden rewards? This trial measures reaction to a known uncertainty; it does not yet establish a meaningful inference system. A later version needs clues that correlate with outcomes and enough repeat examples to test them.

### M10 Multiple stops in one crossing

**Purpose:** Compare itinerary planning with the single-destination loop. At Load, choose one or two different destinations in a fixed order and assign every passenger to exactly one stop. Each booked stop must have at least one assigned soul. All passengers board at the shore, with a combined maximum of four seats. No passengers join during the voyage.

Pay every ordinary route toll plus one extra obol for a second stop before departing. Payments cannot be financed by an earlier stop's fares. One residue may be committed for the entire crossing. All of its Cross effects, including protection and conflict cancellation, apply only to the first leg; any shore-decay prevention lasts until the final Decay step.

Resolve each leg separately: route pressure plus existing wraiths and aboard modifiers, minus eligible protection. Aboard effects consider everyone still on the boat, including souls assigned to a later stop. After surviving the leg, deliver only that stop's assigned souls and collect their rewards. Standing earned here can absorb a later leg's pressure. A Tartarus service payment occurs only when at least one assigned soul arrives there.

After the final stop, resolve one Quota, one Decay, and one Cleanup for the entire crossing. Linked souls receive joint residue only if delivered at the same stop. Partners both aboard but assigned different stops receive solo residue; neither is left on the shore, so no extra separation refusal applies. One partner aboard and one ashore still triggers the base separation rule.

If a later leg fails, keep completed earlier deliveries and payments in history; the run ends before remaining deliveries. No refund applies. New residue is never playable mid-voyage. Waiting souls age only once for the entire itinerary.

**TEAM DECISION:** Does the extra planning justify another pressure resolution? Test this module alone, with no events, conditional gates, destination variants, or changing capacity.

### M11 Promises and commissions

**Purpose:** Add a declared commitment without a new currency. During Load, the player may accept one displayed commission for that crossing. For the trial, the offer exists only when a linked pair is on the shore: deliver both members together on this crossing. Pay no deposit.

Successful joint arrival grants one extra obol, once. If the run survives but the promise is unmet, the next crossing permits no residue play. Place a visible lock token; remove it after that next crossing's Cross step. Cards can still be drawn and discarded while locked. Record fulfillment immediately on joint arrival and preserve it through any later quota failure. A terminal failure before fulfillment records a broken commission but causes no extra playable turn.

A player may decline with no penalty. One accepted promise cannot be changed after confirmation. No offer exists if no linked pair is available; select the lowest-ID pair if two are available. This is a deterministic commissioning rule, not a statement that the ancient gods used these terms.

**TEAM DECISION:** Are promises made to souls, Hades, Persephone, or the river? Should they encourage a new tradeoff rather than pay for something the player would already do?

### M12 NPC encounters and transactions

**Purpose:** Test a brief shore conversation with an explicit mechanical cost. On crossing two, after Recall, an invented underworld clerk offers one of two actions: pay one obol to prevent one chosen waiting soul's normal refusal this crossing, or decline. The offer expires when Load is confirmed.

The payment is included in the confirmation total. The target must remain ashore; changing the load invalidates the offer until another legal target is selected or the service is removed. The clerk does not sell capacity, reveal hidden outcomes, or alter quota. The protection duplicates normal-decay prevention rather than stacking extra refusal removal.

**TEAM DECISION:** Do NPCs belong inside the short shore phase, or in a separate hub? A hub would require its own navigation, inventory, timing, and return rules.

### M13 Difficulty and ending variants

**Learning trial:** Change only starting Standing from three to five. Keep every other rule and the same soul order. **Pressure trial:** Change only starting Standing from three to two. Do not label either balanced. Compare timing, failure crossing, and explained choices using the same measurement sheet.

**Endless-duty concept:** Repeatable duty needs an explicit supply generator, quota schedule, deck-size policy, and voluntary stopping action. It is not enabled by recycling the twelve named souls as if they had never crossed. Use fresh soul instance IDs for new arrivals and keep prior history. Until those four rules are approved, the finite trial remains the executable version.

**Narrative-ending concept:** At a chosen boundary, show factual outcomes such as who travelled together, destinations visited, memories retained, and obligations outstanding. Avoid a total moral score unless the team deliberately wants one. A proposed ending title is a narrative label, not evidence of virtue.

## 20 Greek myth as a design resource

Use ancient stories to generate constraints, requests, and exceptions. Each entry separates a source motif from an invented game rule. The locations in this workbook combine traditions into an adaptation; the team should choose its own coherent version rather than claim one universal ancient map.

### Hermes and the arriving dead

**Ancient passage:** Hermes guides the suitors' spirits toward the asphodel meadow in Homer's Odyssey 24.1 to 14. [G1]

**Game adaptation:** Hermes can announce an approaching group and make relationships visible before boarding. In the deterministic trial, the whole supply is already known, so this is primarily presentation. To test limited forecasting later, hide the unused supply except its next two cards and reveal replacements as cards leave it. Refill still follows section 7; a preview does not add extra arrivals.

**Example line:** Two arrive together. Whether they leave together is yours to decide.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is Hermes a reliable guide, a source of partial information, or an occasional quest giver? Unreliable forecasts need discoverable signals rather than arbitrary deception.

### Styx and promises

**Ancient passage:** Hesiod connects Styx water with divine oaths and punishment for gods who swear falsely, Theogony 775 to 806. [G2]

**Game adaptation:** Use M11's visible promise, payment, and temporary residue lock. The mechanic compresses a mythic idea into a crossing-scale consequence. It does not reproduce the source's divine penalties.

**Example:** An oath marker sits beside the two linked soul IDs. The departure preview states whether the selected load fulfills it. Breaking the promise is possible and legible.

**TEAM DECISION:** Can Charon bind himself, or is he carrying someone else's oath? This affects the player's authority and the meaning of failure.

### Forgetfulness and the value of memory

**Ancient passage:** In Plato's Republic 621a to b, souls reach the Plain of Oblivion and drink forgetful water before rebirth. The passage distinguishes the plain Lethe from the river Ameles. [G3]

**Game adaptation:** M05 turns memory removal into an explicit choice while retaining a record of the person. A permanent version can carry a stronger emotional cost; an archive can test deck management first.

**Example line:** The name will remain in the ledger. The voice need not remain in the boat.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is forgetting mercy, loss, or practical necessity? Do not make deck efficiency the only visible consequence if the fiction asks the player to care about memory.

### Cerberus and the boundary of return

**Ancient passage:** Hesiod's gate hound welcomes entry but prevents escape, Theogony 767 to 774. [G2]

**Game adaptation:** Cerberus can represent the final arrival boundary without becoming a combat boss. In the base trial, the Load confirmation already locks decisions and arrival records are permanent. Introducing a separate reversible approach would add a decision and must define its price and timing.

**Example encounter concept:** A living visitor has permission to enter but lacks permission to leave. Charon's task concerns an exceptional return contract, not defeating the guardian.

**TEAM DECISION:** Does the boat only carry the dead onward, or can special passengers return? Return rules should not silently undo the consequences of ordinary delivery.

### Judges and contested destinations

**Ancient passage:** Plato's Gorgias 523c to 524a describes judgment by Rhadamanthus and Aeacus, with Minos resolving doubtful cases, and routes toward the Isles of the Blessed and Tartarus. [G4]

**Game adaptation:** A soul may have an official assignment, a personal wish, and an unresolved appeal. Those are three different pieces of information, not a single goodness statistic.

**Completed narrative example:** Merchant requests Elysium; an order names Tartarus; a named witness is still waiting. The team can test whether Charon follows the order, waits for testimony, or chooses to defy it. The costs of appeal and defiance are intentionally unapproved. Use M01 alone for the first mechanical wishes test.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is Charon a carrier, guide, advocate, or judge? Resolve this before assigning mandatory destinations across the full catalogue.

### Elysium Asphodel and Tartarus

**Ancient passages:** Odyssey 4.561 to 569 promises Menelaus Elysium through his connection by marriage to Zeus. Odyssey 24 places spirits in asphodel. Hesiod's Theogony 713 to 735 locates imprisoned Titans in Tartarus. [G5; G1; G2]

**Game adaptation:** Preserve meaningful differences among destinations without claiming that every ancient soul followed the same sorting rule. Section 5's preservation, belonging, and reckoning are proposed themes; its prices and pressures are game settings.

**TEAM DECISION:** Which ancient details support the chosen experience, which are changed, and which are omitted? Record the answer in an adaptation ledger rather than relying on a vague claim of accuracy.

### Persephone and a visible calendar

**Ancient passage:** The Homeric Hymn to Demeter assigns Persephone one third of the year below and two thirds with her mother and the other gods, at lines 398 to 403 and 441 to 447. [G6]

**Game adaptation:** A visible schedule can open petitions or special passage. M02 offers a six-crossing access experiment. Its timing is invented and should not be described as a literal seasonal ratio.

**Example concept:** A petition must reach Persephone before a displayed departure date. Waiting for a linked passenger can therefore cost an opportunity.

**TEAM DECISION:** Is the calendar atmospheric, a route restriction, or the main run structure? Hades and Persephone need not have opposing policies unless the team chooses that characterization.

### Orpheus and conditional exceptions

**Ancient passage:** Pseudo-Apollodorus, Library 1.3.2, describes Orpheus receiving conditional permission to recover Eurydice and losing her after looking back. [G7]

**Game adaptation:** An exceptional contract can make information itself tempting. For example, inspecting a sealed passenger's identity could break an explicitly stated condition. This is an encounter concept, not an active trial rule; the team must specify the information benefit and failure outcome before testing it.

**TEAM DECISION:** Use named mythic figures, original souls with similar dilemmas, or both? A literal cameo needs more careful treatment of its source than a broad motif.
